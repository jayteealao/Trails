Counting the street trees of a small city

By Mara Ellison

The survey began with a question nobody at the council could answer: how many trees line our streets? The last count was a typed list from 1987, and half of the streets on it had new names.

How the count worked

Forty volunteers split the city into 212 blocks. Each pair walked one block an evening, measured the trunk of every tree at chest height and noted the species, the state of the bark and the width of the verge.

A volunteer measures a lime on Mill Street.

  • 212 blocks, two walkers each
  • Trunk girth at chest height
  • Species, bark state and verge width

What the trees showed

By the end of the first summer they had 6,400 trees on paper. Lime and plane made up more than half. On the newer estates, cherry and rowan took over, planted in the seventies and now reaching the end of their lives.

The data showed a pattern the walkers had felt on foot: the older centre kept its canopy, while the ring road and the estates beyond it had lost one tree in five since the last count.

Planting from the survey

The council now plants 300 trees a year, chosen from the survey. Each one carries a small tag with a number that matches a row in the open spreadsheet, so anyone can look up its species and its planting date. The spreadsheet is on the council's open data page.
